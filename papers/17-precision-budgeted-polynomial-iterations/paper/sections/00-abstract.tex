\begin{abstract}
Polynomial iterations that map a matrix to the polar factor (Newton--Schulz and minimax
variants, as used for Muon) run in bf16 and fp8, and the shipped designs carry
hand-tuned prescriptions with no account of what they cost at a given precision.  We
give that account in three separable pieces and spend it on the
three prescriptions that are wrong at four bits.
\textbf{Concretely, on the next Muon step:} raise the safety factor from $1.01$ to at
least $1.035$ --- it widens the tolerance of the certified window from $0.8\%$ to $4.8\%$
of its upper edge and lowers the hazard of one Muon call $4$--$200\times$ over
$1{,}200$ steps, which is margin and not immunity: on \texttt{digits} it only delays the
escape, while keeping the state inside its window removes it ($0$ in $210{,}000$ calls);
stop at $K=4$--$5$, since the sixth step spends eight bits for under $1.5\%$ of the window
error; and mix precision only inside a $\sigma_{\max}$-anchored window with
$\ell\le10^{-2}$, where alone it is not Pareto dominated, since at 40 bits a four-step bf16
update errs by $2.4\times10^{-2}$ against $1.4\times10^{-1}$ for the shipped bf16-cast,
two-bf16-step hybrid.
The account: a computable sensitivity indicator $S_{\mathrm{pert}}(K)$ --- a prefix
ratchet of the coefficients' orbit sensitivity, needing no matrix and no iteration ---
forms with one calibration constant $c$ the law
$\mathrm{err} \approx E_K + c\,u\,S_{\mathrm{pert}}(K)$, $u = 2^{-p}$, whose median
predicted/measured ratio over 90 cells is $1.10$ (range $0.76$--$1.46$).  The floor is a
subspace error: the exact iteration adds none of it, the roundings add it at every slot ---
$84$--$86\%$ of it at the first cast of a state whose spectrum still spans $[\ell,1]$, the
rest where each step's sensitivity puts it.  A first-order formula
predicts it within $10\%$ in 52 of 54 square cells and, in the two-term form a rectangular
update requires, within $0.6\%$ in all 500 momentum matrices of a Muon loop, and bounds it
by $O(u/\ell)$; the $u/\ell$ scaling is measured, not proved (a perturbation that only
rescales singular values moves no polar factor), and $\ell^{-0.73}$ is the chord of a
crossover.
Normalising the pair operator by $\sqrt d$ turns the floor's family and dimension
dependence into one number, $6.07 = 1.471\times2\sqrt2\times1.46$: the cast's rms factor
$\sqrt{3/(2\ln2)}$, a derived $2\sqrt2$, and a profile factor of $1.31$ at the median over
180 cells ($0.98$--$2.10$) times the counted multiplicity $1.11$ of the thirteen
injections.  The profile factor is the edge of a four-block variance-profile operator that
reproduces it to $1.2\%$ with no fitted constant, and it follows the decades a spectrum
spans, not $d$.
Inside real training the loss is flat where the update error is not (at twelve bits an
update error of $0.73$ costs $1.65\times$ the loss), and what low precision breaks is
survival, in a single call of the iteration: one rounding lands the state past its window
edge, the quintic's gain then exceeds one ($2.2\times$ at $1.5$ times the edge, $9.3\times$
at twice it) and the update runs to $10^{44}$, while clamping the state inside the window
makes all fifty runs finish and a $10\%$ learning-rate cut leaves seven of eleven failing.
In fp8 the floor matches the unlimited-exponent kernel's at the matched significand to
$1$--$3\%$; online budget adaptation is a negative result (four spectrum-free sensors
cannot see the plateau), and no lower bound on accuracy versus sensitivity holds.
\end{abstract}
